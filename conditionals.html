The table is an assumption.

Four combinations, a bounded result

Each clause has one of two truth values. Two independently assigned clauses give four simultaneous pairs. Philo’s reported criterion excludes only true / false. It does not require a cause, prediction or connection of subject matter.

The finite compatibility test asks whether any admitted pair has a true antecedent and false consequent. The table exhausts its four combinations; it does not establish which combinations the world permits. Choosing a smaller table adds assumptions. In particular, manually excluding “Day, silent” cannot prove that daylight entails talking.

A repeated statement must have the same truth value in both places. That leaves day / day and not-day / not-day. It does not settle what every ancient account calls a sound conditional.

The disagreement stays open

In II.110–114 Sextus reports Philo, Diodorus, proponents of connection, and proponents of potential containment, then challenges how their dispute could be judged. He does not name the connection proponents in this passage. The workshop does not turn his report into an endorsement.

Diodorus’s criterion involves past and present possibility. Observing a few moments, or checking a user-selected finite table, cannot certify it. Potential containment is not defined by the program’s case table either. Neither account receives a computed verdict.

The word “conflicts” in the connection account is represented here by failure of co-admission in the supplied table. That is a modern compatibility analogue. It does not reconstruct ancient relevance, necessity, or the meaning of every conditional in ordinary language.
