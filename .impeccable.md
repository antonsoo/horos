## Design Context

### Users

The owner's brief names fluent English readers who want to learn ancient
philosophy and classical languages through substantial, operable illustrations.
They need no prior knowledge of symbolic logic. Their task here is to keep
premises true while breaking a proposed conclusion, then inspect Aristotle's
words. The owner is unavailable and authorized autonomous design choices.

### Brand Personality

Inquisitive, precise, tactile. A small reasoning studio, with neither a score
nor an illusion that the software judges philosophical truth.

### Aesthetic Direction

An ink-and-vermilion diagram sheet: oversized overlapping circles on a pale
mineral ground, black object counters, generous margins, tightly ruled premise
and conclusion lines. A warm red conclusion is the focal point. Literata for
Greek and headings; Source Sans 3 for controls. No statue stock photos, fake
papyrus, dashboard cards, gradients, decorative charts, or simulated antiquity.
The actual original diagrams are modern tools, explicitly identified as such.

### Design Principles

- Let the learner's objects dominate the first screen.
- Keep every premise readable while the learner changes the world.
- Separate truth in one world from entailment across all permitted worlds.
- Explain Greek direction: 'A belongs to every B' reads as 'Every B is A'.
- Give source text, modern interpretation, and existence assumptions distinct labels.
- Keep token buttons operable with a keyboard and at phone widths; never require dragging.
